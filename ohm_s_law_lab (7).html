1A. Reading the Analog Voltmeter & Ammeter

   
Analog scales have major numbered marks and smaller unnumbered ticks between them. Always check the Least Count (LC) shown at the top-right of each dial:

   
LC = Value of 1 Major Division ÷ Number of Subdivisions

   
    
    
  • Voltmeter 0–3 V: Major marks every 0.5 V with 10 subdivisions → LC = 0.05 V per small tick.
    • 
    
  • Voltmeter 0–10 V: Major marks every 2 V with 10 subdivisions → LC = 0.2 V per small tick.
    • 
    
  • Ammeter 0–100 mA: Major marks every 20 mA with 20 subdivisions → LC = 1 mA per small tick.
    • 
   

   
Tips for Accurate Readings

   
    
    
  • Tap any dial to zoom: Opens a full-screen high-resolution view with fine ±0.1 Ω rheostat buttons right below it.
    • 
    
  • Wait for damping: Real moving-coil needles oscillate briefly before settling. Wait ~1 second until the needle stops moving.
    • 
    
  • Tolerance: Your typed value must be within ±1 LC of the true needle position to be accepted.
    •

  1. E+ ↔ A+: Battery positive terminal to Ammeter positive terminal (series).
    2. 
    
  2. A− ↔ R1: Ammeter negative terminal to the left terminal of unknown Resistance.
    3. 
    
  3. R2 ↔ Rh1: Right terminal of Resistance to the lower fixed terminal of Rheostat Rh.
    4. 
    
  4. Rh2 ↔ K1: Upper sliding wiper terminal of Rheostat to right terminal of Plug Key (K).
    5. 
    
  5. K2 ↔ E−: Left terminal of Plug Key (K) back to Battery negative terminal.
    6. 
    
  6. V+ ↔ R1 (Conn. A) or A+ (Conn. B): Voltmeter positive terminal to the high-potential side.
    7. 
    
  7. V− ↔ R2: Voltmeter negative terminal to the right terminal of Resistance.
    8. 
   

   
Trial-and-Error Learning: If you drag between two wrong terminals (such as short-circuiting E+ to E−, reversing meter polarity, or putting Ammeter in parallel), the circuit shows a red fault wire and explains the exact physics error.

   
   
Why Connection A and Connection B Differ

   
Real meters are not ideal (Voltmeter resistance Rv is finite; Ammeter resistance Ra > 0):

   
    
    
  • Connection A (V across R only): Voltmeter draws a tiny current, so the ammeter reads slightly more than the current in R. Measured V/I = R ∥ Rv (slightly lower than true R).
    • 
    
  • Connection B (V across A + R): Voltmeter measures voltage across both the ammeter and wire. Measured V/I = R + Ra (slightly higher than true R).
    • 
   

  

 


 

  
2. Mastering the Screw Gauge (Micrometer)

  
The screw gauge measures very thin wires to 0.01 mm precision. Its pitch is 1 mm (1 full thimble rotation moves the spindle 1 mm) and the circular thimble has 100 divisions, so LC = 1 mm / 100 = 0.01 mm.

  

   

    
Step A: Find Zero Error (Before Inserting Wire)

    
    
     
  1. Make sure no wire is in the jaws (button says Insert wire).
    2. 
     
  2. Slide the thimble left (or click -10) until the jaws close and "Ratchet clicking" appears.
    3. 
     
  3. Look at which circular thimble division aligns with the horizontal red reference line:
      
      
       
    • Positive Zero Error (CSR ≤ 50): The 0 mark is below the red line.
      Zero Error = +(CSR × 0.01) mm
      • 
       
    • Negative Zero Error (CSR > 50): The 0 mark is above the red line (e.g., 97 means 3 divisions short of 100).
      Zero Error = −((100 − CSR) × 0.01) mm
      • 
      
    
     
    4. 
     
  4. Enter the signed value (e.g., +0.03 or -0.02) and click Check zero error.
    5. 
    

   

   

    
Step B: Measure the Wire Diameter

    
    
     
  1. Open the thimble (slide right), click Insert wire, then tighten left until the ratchet clicks.
    2. 
     
  2. MSR (Main Scale Reading): Read the last whole millimeter mark visible on the horizontal sleeve (0, 1, 2... mm).
    3. 
     
  3. CSR (Circular Scale Reading): Read the thimble division (0–99) aligned with the red line.
    4. 
     
  4. Calculate Observed & Corrected diameter:
      
    Observed = MSR + (CSR × 0.01) mm
    Corrected d = Observed − (Zero Error)
    
      Watch the minus sign! Subtracting a negative zero error adds to the observed reading (e.g., 0.45 − (-0.02) = 0.47 mm).
     
    5.

3A. Metre Scale & Sliding Magnifier

   
    
    
  • The copper wire rests along a 0–100 cm ruler, starting at a random offset (not at 0 cm) to prevent worn-end error.
    • 
    
  • The top bar shows the full 100 cm overview; the bottom bar shows a 4 cm zoomed window inside the red rectangle.
    • 
    
  • Slide the magnifier over the left end of the wire, read to the nearest 0.1 cm (1 mm tick), then slide to the right end and read again.
    • 
    
  • Total wire length is simply: L = Right End − Left End (in cm).
    • 
   

  


  

   
3B. Calculating Specific Resistance (ρ)

   
Resistivity (ρ) is an intrinsic property of the wire's alloy, independent of its thickness or length:

   
ρ = (R × A) / L   where   A = πd² / 4

   
    
    
  • Automatic Unit Conversion: When you click Calculate ρ, the app automatically converts mean diameter d from mm → m (×10−3) and length L from cm → m (×10−2).
    • 
    
  • Click Use slope of my V–I graph to transfer your resistance R directly from Tab 1.
    • 
    
  • Compare your calculated ρ (in Ω·m) against the 5 standard alloys listed at the bottom of the card.
    • 
   

  

 


 

  
4. Troubleshooting & Common Mistakes

  

   "Needles still swinging – wait."
   
Whenever you close Key K, change Battery E, or move the Rheostat, the analog needles oscillate with realistic inertia. Wait about 1 second until both needles come to rest before clicking Check & record.

  

  

   "Jaws too close – open the thimble first."
   
In the Screw Gauge, you cannot insert a wire if the gap between the anvil and spindle is narrower than the wire's diameter. Drag the thimble slider to the right (or press +10) to open the jaws, then press Insert wire.

  

  

   "Re-read: error must be ≤ 1 LC."
   
Your entered reading is slightly off from the needle or scale mark. Tap the meter, screw gauge, or metre scale canvas to open the full-screen zoom view and count the minor subdivisions carefully.

  

  

   Why doesn't my V–I slope match "Reveal R" to 100% precision?
   
Because the simulation includes realistic voltmeter resistance (Rv) and ammeter resistance (Ra)! In Connection A, the voltmeter draws a small parallel current; in Connection B, the ammeter adds a small series resistance. Use a higher voltmeter range (larger Rv) in Connection A to get closer to the true R.
